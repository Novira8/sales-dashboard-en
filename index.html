Sales to retail chains, 2017–2020

A household goods supplier: 8 retail chains and smaller customers, 218 products, July 2017 – November 2020.

All amounts in euros, excluding VAT (the source table includes 20% VAT, divided by 1.2). December 2020 is not closed and is left out of the totals. Demo data: chain names are replaced with numbers, smaller customers are anonymised.

1. Sales by month

Peaks in December and January, a dip in spring and summer, a sharp drop in April–July 2020.

How it's calculated: the sum of the REVENUE column over all product rows of the month's main sheet, divided by 1.2. Sheet copies, total and service rows are skipped. Each month is checked against the sheet's own total: it matches in 41 months out of 42; in July 2017 the sheet total itself is 0.

2. Top products for the whole closed period

By units, cheap items lead (microwave covers, bag clips); by revenue, shoe dryers take 4th, 6th, 7th and 10th place.

By units

By revenue, € excl. VAT

How it's calculated: a product is identified by its name in the accounting system, because every chain uses its own barcode for the same product. Units = sum of “Sales, units”, revenue = sum of REVENUE / 1.2, July 2017 – November 2020. Similar names (for example, shoe dryer “Universal” and “Universal, appliance”) are different products, as confirmed by the data owner.

3. Average revenue per chain per month

The average is per chain: how much one chain brings in a month on average.

Revenue per active chain

Revenue from each customer for the whole period, € excl. VAT

How it's calculated: the month's revenue excl. VAT divided by the number of customers with revenue above zero in that month. Departments of one chain (Chain 1 – 10/30/50/100, Chain 6 – 10/30/50) are merged into one chain. The table has no orders, so an average order value can't be calculated.

Returns were handled as a 1-for-1 exchange: the customer got the same product, no money was refunded. So returns don't reduce revenue, and there is no separate chart.

How it's calculated: the sum of rows with negative revenue or negative units for the closed period (July 2017 – November 2020). Such rows are subtracted from revenue and the top lists. The 1-for-1 exchange rule was confirmed by the data owner.

5. Year over year

Each line is one year, so you can see whether the same month was better or worse a year earlier.

201820192020

How it's calculated: monthly revenue excl. VAT. “vs previous month” = (month / previous month − 1), “vs a year ago” = (month / same month last year − 1). December 2020 is not closed and not shown.

6. Money received from the chains

This is what the owner counted as a sale: money in the bank. The table records it as one amount per month, and not for every month.

ReceivedNeeds checking: same as last month or looks like an error

How it's calculated: row 2 of the “ACTUALLY RECEIVED” column on the month's main sheet, divided by 1.2. Before 2018 and from April 2020 the column is empty or has a formula error. Grey marks months where the amount equals the previous month to the cent, and January 2018 (74k against revenue of 835k). Payments are not split by chain, so all blocks above use revenue from the chains' reports.

7. Data issues

How it's handled: rows where something is missing or a number cell contains text. No row is dropped. No product name — the name from the chain's report is used. No chain — revenue stays in the month but the row isn't counted as a chain. Empty revenue with a price and units — calculated as price × units. Text instead of a number — counted as 0 and listed here. Customer names are not shown: some of them are names of private individuals.
